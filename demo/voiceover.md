# AirCast demo video — recording guide

**How to record:** open `demo/video.html` in a browser (or the published page),
go fullscreen, start a screen recording with microphone (QuickTime: File → New
Screen Recording, pick your mic), press **Play**, and read the lines below as
each scene appears. Total runtime ≈ 105 s — under the 2-minute limit. Space
pauses if you fall behind; R restarts for another take. One clean take is
plenty; the brief says an unedited screen recording is fine.

Suggested split: **Parth** reads scenes 1–4, **Anay** reads scenes 5–7 (or swap).

---

**Scene 1 — The day (0:00–0:12)**
> On June 27th, 2023, Chicago woke up to some of the worst air in the world —
> 134 micrograms of fine-particle pollution. The day before? Nine.
> Nobody saw it coming.

**Scene 2 — The decision (0:12–0:23)**
> We built AirCast for one person: a school athletics director deciding *today*
> whether tomorrow's practice should be outside. In Chicago, one day in nine is
> over the WHO guideline — and weather apps only tell you about right now.

**Scene 3 — How it works (0:23–0:41)**
> AirCast predicts tomorrow's PM2.5 as the EPA's own monitors will measure it.
> It uses twenty-five public signals — today's monitor readings, weather, and
> our favorite: air quality right now in four cities upwind, because the air
> over Minneapolis today is over Chicago tomorrow. A ridge regression model,
> trained on the past, tested only on the future.

**Scene 4 — Live demo (0:41–0:59)**
> And here it is, live. One command — it fetches current conditions in Chicago
> and all four upwind cities, and in a few seconds: tomorrow's forecast, an
> uncertainty range, and a clear answer. Today — all clear.

**Scene 5 — Results (0:59–1:19)**
> Does it actually beat a simple guess? On 315 days of 2025 the model never saw:
> ten percent more accurate than persistence — twenty-five percent at three days
> out. Our WATCH alert caught 88 percent of bad days a day in advance, and when
> we issued a warning, we were right 77 percent of the time.

**Scene 6 — Honesty (1:19–1:35)**
> And here's where it fails. We replayed June 2023 with a model trained only on
> the past. Nothing on the ground sees the first smoke day coming — we predicted
> seventeen, reality was one-thirty-four. Right decision, wrong magnitude.
> Extreme smoke needs satellite data, and we measured that gap instead of
> hiding it.

**Scene 7 — Close (1:35–1:45)**
> AirCast. Honest forecasts for the days that are forecastable — and honesty
> about the days that aren't. Thanks for watching.
